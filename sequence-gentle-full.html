Forty Gentle Minutes

Twenty-two entries: warm-up, standing work, floor work, then five minutes lying still. This is the shape of a studio class without the drive there, paced so that you can set a pose up properly instead of landing in it and hoping. It is the longest thing on the site and the only one with a full arc.

Difficulty
Beginner to intermediate
Props required
A mat, two blocks, a strap, one folded blanket, a clear stretch of wall
Props optional
A bolster, a second blanket, a sturdy chair
Breath pattern
Even breath during standing work. Inhale 4, exhale 6 on the floor.
Structure
8 minutes warm-up, 15 minutes standing, 12 minutes floor, 5 minutes rest
Before you start

This practice assumes you have already met the ten-minute morning sequence a few times, and that Downward-Facing Dog and Warrior II are shapes you recognise rather than read about. If that is not you yet, go and do that one for a few weeks. Nothing at all is lost by arriving here in a month.

Timed order

Pose-by-pose order

  1. Seated centering Sukhāsana Sit on the folded blanket so the hips finish higher than the knees. Inhale four, exhale four. Two minutes of sitting still is longer than it reads on paper, and that is the whole reason it is first.
    2 minutes
  2. Reclined knee hugs Apānāsana Lie on your back and draw both knees toward the chest. Circle them slowly, small at first, in both directions. The lower back is waking up, so let it be dull rather than interesting.
    90 seconds
  3. Cat and Cow Mārjaryāsana and Bitilāsana On hands and knees with the blanket folded under them. Move at breath speed for the full two minutes. Two minutes at breath speed is roughly fifteen rounds, which is fewer than most people expect.
    2 minutes
  4. Child's Pose Bālāsana Knees as wide as the mat, hips travelling back, forehead on the floor or on a block. Three minutes here is the first real rest of the practice, not a pause between two better things. Full guide.
    3 minutes
  5. Downward-Facing Dog Adho Mukha Śvānāsana Knees generously bent, spine long. Spend the first half walking the heels alternately if that suits you, then settle. Straight legs are not the goal and never were. Full guide.
    2 minutes
  6. Standing Forward Fold Uttānāsana Walk the feet toward the hands and hang. Knees soft, head heavy, hands on blocks or sliding down the shins. Let the neck go before you decide you have finished.
    90 seconds
  7. Mountain Pose Tāḍāsana Roll up one vertebra at a time, head last. Then stand still with the feet hip-width and find out where your weight actually sits. That reading is what the standing work is measured against.
    90 seconds
  8. Warrior II, right foot forward Vīrabhadrāsana II Wide stance. Front knee tracking over the second toe, back outer heel pressed down, torso stacked upright between the legs rather than leaning toward the front foot. Full guide.
    90 seconds
  9. Warrior II, left foot forward Vīrabhadrāsana II Second side. Match the stance width and the depth of the knee bend to what you did on the right, not to what this side would prefer.
    90 seconds
  10. Triangle, right Utthita Trikoṇāsana Straighten the front leg without locking the knee, then tip sideways over it from the hip. Lower hand onto a block, inside or outside the shin, whichever one lets the chest stay open.
    60 seconds
  11. Triangle, left Utthita Trikoṇāsana Second side. The block may well want a different height here than it did on the right. Use the height that works and do not make it mean anything.
    60 seconds
  12. Tree Pose, right foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana Stand beside the wall with two fingertips resting on it. The lifted foot goes to the ankle, the calf or the inner thigh, and never onto the side of the knee. Full guide.
    60 seconds
  13. Tree Pose, left foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana Second side. One leg is almost always steadier than the other. That gap closes over weeks of standing on both, not over the next sixty seconds.
    60 seconds
  14. Low Lunge, right foot forward Añjaneyāsana Back knee down on the folded blanket, hands on blocks either side of the front foot. Let the hips settle forward and the chest stay up. Ninety seconds is long enough that it will change under you.
    90 seconds
  15. Low Lunge, left foot forward Añjaneyāsana Second side, and the last of the standing work. Take it slowly. Everything after this happens sitting down or lying flat.
    90 seconds
  16. Seated Forward Fold Paścimottānāsana Sit on the front edge of the folded blanket with the strap around the balls of both feet and the knees softly bent. Inhale to lengthen, exhale to travel a little further. Three minutes is long enough that you should not arrive on the first breath. Full guide.
    3 minutes
  17. Seated twist, right Ardha Matsyendrāsana Still on the blanket, both sitting bones down. Inhale to lengthen, exhale to turn, and let the turn start at the base of the spine so the head is the last thing that moves.
    90 seconds
  18. Seated twist, left Ardha Matsyendrāsana Second side. The hands rest where they land. They are not there to crank you round another few degrees.
    90 seconds
  19. Bridge Pose Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana Feet hip-width and close enough that your fingertips just graze your heels. The lift comes from the legs, not from squeezing the lower back. Once you are up, the head stays where it is. Full guide.
    90 seconds
  20. Reclined twist, right Supta Matsyendrāsana Let the knees travel to the right, onto a block or bolster if they need it, so that both shoulders can stay on the floor. The shoulder is the priority, not how far the knees go.
    2 minutes
  21. Reclined twist, left Supta Matsyendrāsana Second side. When the count runs out, take your time arranging the blanket and anything else you want for the final rest. Setting it up is part of it.
    2 minutes
  22. Final rest Śavāsana Lie flat with a rolled blanket under the knees, arms a little away from the sides, palms up. Put a layer over you if the room has cooled. Five minutes, doing nothing, which is an eighth of the practice and the part most likely to get cut.
    5 minutes

Every body

Beginner modification path

Run this column the first several times through, or on any day you want the shape of the practice without the standing demand. Same twenty-two entries, same order, same hold times.

  • Reclined knee hugs: keep the feet on the floor and draw one knee at a time toward the chest.
  • Cat and Cow: do it seated on a chair with the hands on the thighs if hands and knees is not comfortable.
  • Child's Pose: use a bolster lengthwise between the knees, or fold forward over a cushion while seated on a chair.
  • Downward-Facing Dog: use the wall version. Hands flat on the wall at hip height, feet walked back until the torso is horizontal.
  • Standing Forward Fold: fold only halfway, hands on the thighs, back flat.
  • Warrior II: bend the front knee to forty-five degrees rather than ninety, or use the seated chair version.
  • Triangle: rest the lower hand on the seat of a chair placed beside the front foot rather than on a block.
  • Tree Pose: use the kickstand version with the toes of the lifted foot still on the floor, fingertips on the wall.
  • Low Lunge: stand facing a chair, one foot on the seat, both hands on the chair back.
  • Seated Forward Fold: sit on two folded blankets, use the strap, and keep the knees well bent.
  • Bridge Pose: lift the hips only an inch or two, or place a block under the pelvis and rest on it instead.
  • Final rest: put a bolster under the knees and a folded blanket under the head. If lying flat is not comfortable, rest on your left side with a cushion between the knees.

01 / Afterwards

The twenty-four hours after

How this practice should feel the next morning

Forty minutes is long enough to leave something behind, and people who are new to a practice this length often cannot tell whether what they are feeling the next day is ordinary. Here is the honest range.

Ordinary

Broad, dull, even soreness in the front of the hips, the backs of the legs and the outer shoulders, arriving the next morning rather than that night. It should feel like the muscle has been used, it should be roughly the same on both sides, and it should ease as you move around during the day rather than sharpen.

Worth noticing

Soreness that is clearly worse on one side, or that is still there on the third day. Neither is an emergency. Both usually mean one side worked harder to make up for something, which is worth taking to a teacher who can watch you do Warrior II rather than something to solve alone on a mat.

Not ordinary

Anything sharp, anything in a joint rather than a muscle, and anything involving tingling, numbness or weakness. That is not soreness and it is not something to practice through or wait out. Leave the sequence alone and talk to a qualified healthcare professional.

Nothing at all

Also a completely normal result, and not a sign the practice failed. A gentle forty minutes is meant to leave you able to do it again. If you want more from it, hold the standing poses for their full count rather than adding poses or going deeper.

  • Next morning Dull and even, easing as you move. Expected.
  • Day three Still sore, or sore on one side only. Ask a teacher.
  • Sharp or numb Not soreness. Stop and ask a professional.

Once a week, on the day you are least rushed, will do more for you than three attempts crammed into a bad one.

02 / In practice

Field notes

Notes on practicing this one

Forty minutes has to be defended before it starts. Phone in another room, or at least silent. Two blocks, the strap and the blanket laid out at the top of the mat. The most common way a long practice at home ends early is a notification arriving somewhere in the middle of the seated forward fold.

The five minutes at the end are structural. They are an eighth of the practice, and they are the first thing people cut when the clock looks unfriendly. If watching the time is what makes you cut it, set an alarm for the five minutes and then stop looking.

The sides will not match, and that is not a defect. Eight of these poses are done twice, and you will notice a difference in most of them. Some of that is habit, some of it is how you are built, and none of it is a problem to fix while you are in the pose. Notice it and carry on.

Once a week is the right frequency. This is not a daily practice. A week that works looks like three or four ten-minute mornings, one or two desk resets, one evening wind-down, and this one on whichever day is least crowded.

Print this one especially. Twenty-two entries are more than anyone wants to carry in their head while also keeping the breath even. A sheet of paper at the edge of the mat removes the question of what comes next, and it keeps the screen out of the room.
