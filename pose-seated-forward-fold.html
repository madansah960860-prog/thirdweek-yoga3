Seated fold

Seated Forward Fold

Paścimottānāsana  (POSH-chee-moh-tah-NAH-suh-nuh)

Legs long in front of you, hinge forward from the hips. Paścima means west, which in the older texts stands for the back of the body: the side facing the setting sun when you turn east at dawn. More people practise this shape backwards than any other in the library, and two props, a blanket and a strap, correct nearly all of it.

The whole pose

Set-up, cues and every version

Paścimottānāsana

  • Intermediate
  • Hamstrings
  • Spine
  • Strap recommended
Difficulty
Intermediate. Simply sitting upright with the legs long is the first hurdle, and for many people the only one that matters this year.
Breath pattern
Inhale 4 counts to lengthen. Exhale 6 counts to travel. Never fold on an inhale.
Suggested hold
1 to 2 minutes, or 5 to 8 breaths if the practice is moving
What moves
Flexion at the hip, length through the hamstrings and calves, lengthening along the whole back of the body
Props that help
A strap, one or two folded blankets to sit on, a bolster across the thighs, a chair
Where it appears
Gentle Full Practice
Entry steps
  • Sit with both legs stretched out in front of you, feet hip-width apart.
  • Check what your pelvis does. If your lower back rolls backward the second you sit down, sit up on the front edge of one or two folded blankets until upright costs you nothing. For most people this step is not optional.
  • Flex the feet gently so the toes point up and both kneecaps face the ceiling.
  • Leave a soft bend in the knees. Straight legs are not an entry requirement here, and bending them is not a compromise.
  • Loop a strap around the balls of both feet and take an end in each hand.
  • Inhale for 4 counts and lift through the crown of your head until the spine is as long as it goes.
  • Exhale for 6 counts and hinge forward from the hip joints, walking your hands down the strap only as far as that length survives.
  • Stop the moment your lower back starts to round. That exact point is the pose. Stay there.
Alignment cues
  • Hinge at the hip, not at the waist. Put your attention on the crease where your thigh meets your torso and let the fold happen there.
  • Chest toward the feet, not nose toward knees. Leading with your head is what produces the rounded version everybody recognises.
  • Sit bones back and down. The pelvis has to tip forward for this fold to come from the hips at all. If it will not tip, you are sitting too low.
  • Keep the knees soft. Bending them frees the pelvis to tip. Straightening them at the cost of your spine trades the working part of the pose for a picture of it.
  • Shoulders down and back. Hauling on the strap with the shoulders up around your ears adds nothing except tension in your neck.
  • Travel on the exhale. Inhale to lengthen, exhale to move. Never the reverse.
Common mistakes
  • Sitting flat on the floor with the pelvis rolled back. The single most common error on this page. Almost everybody needs to sit on something, and there is nothing to read into that.
  • Grabbing the feet and hauling. Pulling yourself forward by the toes rounds the spine and quietly converts a hip hinge into spinal flexion, which is a different pose with different risks.
  • Locking the knees straight. Straight legs are a result here, never an instruction. A locked knee stops the pelvis tipping, which stops the pose.
  • Bouncing deeper. Pulsing at the bottom is not how tissue takes on length, and it does raise your chances of overdoing it.
  • Measuring the pose by how far your hands reach. Reach is mostly a fact about the length of your bones. It tells you next to nothing about how well the pose is going.
  • Losing the breath at the bottom. If the breath stops, come up an inch. It will start again, and that inch is where the pose was.
Accessible modifications

All four are the pose. The bolster version is what a restorative teacher would hand you, and it earns five uninterrupted minutes.

Strap around the feet

Loop a 6-foot strap around the balls of both feet, take an end in each hand and keep the elbows soft. The strap lends your arms a length they do not have, which means your spine is never asked to round in order to reach. A bathrobe belt or a long towel does exactly the same job.

Sitting on blankets

Sit on the front edge of one or two firmly folded blankets so your hips sit higher than your knees. That lift is what lets the pelvis tip forward, and the pelvis tipping forward is the entire mechanism of this pose. Two to six inches covers most people. Needing more than that is a fact about your proportions, not a verdict on you.

Bolster on the thighs

Lay a bolster or a stack of folded blankets across your thighs and rest your torso and forehead down onto it. Nothing is being held up by you. Stay three to five minutes with a six-count exhale. In an evening practice, this is the version to reach for.

Seated in a chair

Sit forward on a sturdy chair with both feet flat and hip-width apart. Inhale to lengthen, exhale and hinge forward over your thighs, letting your hands and forearms land on your thighs, on a second chair, or on a stack of books. Same hinge at the hip, same breath counts, and no getting down to the floor.

Progression

You progress here by lowering what you sit on, not by reaching further forward. When you can sit upright on a single folded blanket with the pelvis level, try a thinner fold, then the bare floor. Only once that is comfortable does working on the depth of the fold make any sense. The next pose along is Head-to-Knee Forward Bend, the same hinge with one leg folded in, which lets you work one side at a time and almost always exposes a difference between them.

Contraindications and cautions

Take care, keep the knees well bent, or take the chair version if you have a disc injury or a history of lower back injury, sciatic symptoms, a recent hamstring strain, or a hip injury or replacement. In pregnancy, take the legs wider so the front of your body has room. Come out if you feel sharp sensation behind a knee, at the sitting bones, or anything that travels down the back of the leg.

The set-up that decides everything

Finding your sitting height

Get this one measurement right and most of the page takes care of itself. Get it wrong and no amount of good cueing will save the pose. It takes about ninety seconds to work out, once, and then you know it.

  1. Sit on the bare floor and look down

    Legs long, knees soft. Put a hand behind you on the floor and feel what your lower back is doing. If it has rolled backward into a C, the floor is too low for you today. Almost everyone finds this, including people who have practised for years.

  2. Add height until the pelvis stands up

    Fold a firm blanket and sit on its front edge, not its middle. Keep adding folds, an inch at a time, until you can sit tall with your hips above your knees and your lower back holding its natural inward curve without you working for it.

  3. Test it with one small hinge

    Inhale for 4 counts, then exhale for 6 and tip forward two inches from the hip crease. If your lower back stays long through those two inches, your height is right. If it rounds straight away, add another inch and test again.

  4. Write the number down

    Two blankets, or one blanket and a thin cushion, or a bolster on its side. Whatever it turned out to be, remember it, so that next time you set up in ten seconds instead of ninety.

  5. Expect it to change

    Sitting height is not a permanent score. It moves with the time of day, with how long you have been in a car, and with how much walking you did yesterday. Check it on any day the pose feels unfamiliar.

  • Breath Inhale 4 counts to lengthen, exhale 6 to travel.
  • Hold One to two minutes, or five to eight breaths in a moving practice.
  • Props A strap for your arms, folded blankets for your pelvis. Both, not one.

On reaching

Your hands do not have to reach your feet

How far forward you travel in this pose is settled by a handful of things you had no say in: the length of your thigh bones relative to your torso, how deep your hip sockets are, the shape of the top of the femur, and how far your pelvis tips before your spine has to take over. Two people with identical hamstring length can finish eight inches apart, and both of them are doing it correctly.

None of that alters what the pose is for. The working part is the hinge at the hip with a long spine, held with a steady breath. That is available at three inches of travel and at eighteen. It disappears the moment the spine rounds to buy extra distance, which is, inconveniently, the exact moment the pose starts to look more impressive.

A strap is the cheapest way to keep the honest version. It removes the reason to round, because your arms are suddenly long enough. What you give up is the image of a body folded in half, and that image was never the thing doing the work.

Stop at the point where your lower back would start to round. That point is the pose, at three inches or eighteen.
