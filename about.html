Behind the guides

How this site is written, and who pays for it

Stillform publishes free yoga instruction in plain American English. This page covers the rules the writing has to pass before it goes up, the things we refuse to claim, who the site is for, and every source of money that touches it.

Written, not filmed

Why this site is words

Most yoga instruction online is a video you have to keep pausing with wet hands, or a page that says "hold for a few breaths" and moves on. Neither helps at nine at night in a living room when the actual question is what your back foot is supposed to be doing.

So Stillform is written down. Every pose carries entry steps, alignment cues, a breath pattern with counts, a hold time in seconds, the mistakes people make in practice, at least two prop-supported options presented as equals rather than fallbacks, one progression, and a note on who should leave it alone. Every sequence states its length, its difficulty and its prop list, then gives the poses in order with a hold on each and a written beginner path running alongside.

The name is a description of the thing itself. A shape, held still, long enough to become something you recognize. The number beside a pose is what separates instruction from a suggestion.

Credentials

Who teaches here

Credential notice

The profile below is a template. The name, the training, the credentials and the biography are placeholders, and they have to be replaced with a real, checkable teacher before this site goes live. Stillform does not publish invented credentials. Nothing in the bracketed text below describes any real person.

[Teacher name]

Training: [Name of the training program, the lead trainers, the number of hours and the years attended] · Registration: [Registering body and registration number, if any] · Teaching since: [Year]

[Two or three paragraphs of biography. How they came to the practice, which lineages they trained in, what they teach now and where, and what they are most interested in teaching. Written in the first person or the third, consistently.]

[A paragraph about what they do not do: the formats they do not teach, the questions they refer elsewhere, and the boundary between yoga teaching and healthcare.]

What a teaching credential is, and what it is not. A yoga teaching qualification is a record that somebody finished a course of training. That is the whole of it. It is not a healthcare qualification. A yoga teacher is not a physician, a physical therapist, a chiropractor or a clinician of any kind, and nothing on this site is written by one acting in that capacity. A question about a condition, an injury or a medication belongs with a qualified healthcare professional, and we will say so every time.

Five steps, same order

How a pose guide gets written

Nothing here is written in one sitting. Every pose guide goes through the same five stages in the same sequence, and a draft that fails one of them goes back rather than forward.

  1. Practice it, teach it, then write it

    No guide is written from a book alone. A pose is practiced across a range of setups, taught to people at different levels of experience, and only written up afterwards. That order is why the common-mistakes section reads the way it does: it comes from watching people make the mistakes, not from a published list of them.

  2. Draft the modifications before the full pose

    The chair, wall and block versions are written first, then the unsupported one. The order is not an accident. Modifications written last always read like apologies, and a reader can hear it. Written first, they read like what they are: one of several correct ways to do the pose.

  3. Strip out every claim

    A separate editing pass exists purely to delete anything that says or hints that a pose treats, relieves, cures, prevents or improves a condition. What is left describes what physically occurs: which joints move, in which direction, which tissue lengthens, and what has to work to hold you up.

  4. Check the Sanskrit against the standard

    Names are set with their diacritics and checked against standard transliteration rather than copied from another website. The pronunciation guides beside them are approximations written in ordinary American English, and every one of them says so.

  5. Fix it in public when it is wrong

    Tell us something here is inaccurate and it gets corrected, with a dated note on the page saying what changed and when. Corrections are not slipped in quietly and the old wording is not pretended away. The contact page has the address for exactly this.

The house rules

The standards every page is held to

The process above says how a guide is built. This is the specification it has to meet before it goes up, written out so you can check our work against it.

Reading level
Plain American English, aimed at a comfortable read for a general adult reader. Any technical word is defined the first time it appears.
Second pair of eyes
No pose guide publishes on one person's say-so. A second teacher reads it against the pose before it goes live.
Review cycle
Every pose guide and sequence is re-read at least once a year, and the review date is printed on the page.
Corrections
Fixed within two business days of being reported, with a dated note on the page saying what changed.
Images
Licensed or original. No image is presented as a demonstration of a pose unless it actually shows that pose.
Sponsorship
A sponsor never sees a draft, never approves copy, and never appears in instructional text.

If we cannot say where a claim came from, the claim comes out. That rule has removed more sentences from this site than any other.

The editing pass, in one line

Something here reading as vague, overstated or simply wrong is worth telling us about. Write to hello@stillform.co and put the page name in the subject line.

Advertisement
Reserved 728 × 90 display slot. Space is held at all breakpoints so nothing on the page moves when an ad loads.

Our limits

Six things you will never read here

Set down in public so you can hold us to it later.

  • Health, medical or therapeutic claims. No pose, sequence or breathing pattern on this site is described as treating, curing, healing, relieving or preventing anything. Not back pain, not anxiety, not sleep, not digestion, not blood pressure, not immunity, not fertility. We write what happens in the body physically, and then we stop writing.
  • Weight-loss framing. No calorie counts, no fat-burning language, no transformations, no before-and-after photographs, no toning, no remarks about the shape of anybody's body. Yoga is not offered here as a way to change how you look.
  • Diet content of any kind. No cleanses, no fasting, no detox, no supplements, no meal plans. This is a movement education site and that is the entire scope of it.
  • Invented credentials, reviews or testimonials. Any teacher name, qualification, review or rating that has not been verified is marked as a placeholder in the page itself and never dressed up as real.
  • Manufactured urgency. No countdown clocks, no invented scarcity, no expiring discount, no line telling you three places are left. Nothing here is going anywhere.
  • Unlabeled sponsorship. An article a brand paid for says so above the headline, in the first line you read, not in a footnote underneath.

Honest fit

Who this is for, and who it is not

A site that claims to suit everyone usually suits nobody in particular. Stillform is written with one reader in mind, and being clear about that is more useful to you than pretending otherwise.

Written for you if

You are starting from nothing, or starting again after years away. You would rather read an instruction than pause a video with one hand. You practice at home, alone, in whatever space is free. You want the chair and wall options treated as real options rather than as concessions. You want to know how long to hold something and why. You are looking for a first studio class and want to know what happens in the room before you walk into it.

Not written for you if

You want video classes to follow in real time; there is no video here and there are sites that do it well. You are looking for advanced postures, arm balances or inversions; we index them and send you to a teacher. You want yoga prescribed for a diagnosis, an injury or a pregnancy; that requires a clinician who can see you. You want teacher-training material. You want a program that promises a result by a date. None of that is what this is.

If you are in the second column, we would rather say so now than take up your afternoon. The teacher directory is usually the better next step.

Six sources, no seventh

Every way this site makes money

All of it is free to read, and that has to be paid for somehow. Here is the complete list. If a seventh ever appears it will be added here first.

  • Display advertising. One labeled slot per page, always below the content you came for. None on the legal pages and none on the 404 page. No advertising cookie is set until you have agreed to it.
  • Membership, $6 a month or $54 a year in US dollars. It clears the advertising and adds downloadable sequence PDFs, saved practice collections, printable pose cards and early sight of new guides. It has never moved a free instruction behind a wall, and that is the one commitment we would be most embarrassed to break.
  • Affiliate commissions on mats, blocks, straps, blankets and bolsters. The disclosure sits beside the link you are looking at rather than only in the footer, and every prop we recommend also lists a free household substitute.
  • Paid digital products, currently the downloadable beginner program and the printable sequence bundles.
  • Sponsored content and brand partnerships, labeled sponsored above the headline. We turn down sponsorship from supplement, diet, cleanse and weight-loss businesses, and we do not run sponsored pieces that contain health claims, whatever the fee.
  • The teacher directory, where a basic listing costs nothing and a featured placement is paid for and says featured on it.

The full detail, including the affiliate language the FTC requires, is on the Advertising and Affiliate Disclosure page. Membership terms are on the Pricing page.
