Health and safety disclaimer

Read this before you practice anything on this site. It is linked from every pose guide and every sequence, not only from the footer, because it matters more than most of what is written here.

Effective date: August 27, 2026  ·  Last updated: September 14, 2026

In plain English

Stillform publishes general movement education. It is not medical advice and it is not a substitute for professional healthcare. Consult a qualified healthcare professional before beginning any new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical, or managing a health condition. If something hurts, stop. For hands-on guidance, work with a qualified teacher in the room with you.

That is a summary, not the disclaimer. The sections below say it in full, including the symptoms that mean stop immediately and seek care.

  • Not medical advice Nothing here diagnoses, treats or manages any condition, and we make no health claims.
  • Ask first Speak with a healthcare professional before you start, especially if section 3 describes you.
  • Pain is a stop signal Come out of the pose immediately, not at the end of the count.

1. This is not medical advice

We would rather be blunt about this than reassuring. Everything on this site is general education about physical movement. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, physical therapy, or professional healthcare of any kind. Reading this site creates no practitioner relationship of any sort between you and Stillform.

The people who write this site are movement teachers, not physicians, physical therapists, chiropractors, athletic trainers or clinicians of any description, and nothing here is written in a clinical capacity. Any teaching credential shown anywhere on this site is currently a placeholder awaiting replacement with a real, verifiable qualification, and a yoga teaching credential is in any case not a healthcare qualification.

2. We make no health claims

Stillform does not state or imply that any pose, sequence, breathing pattern or practice described here treats, cures, heals, relieves, prevents, manages or improves any medical condition.

That includes, and is not limited to: back pain, neck pain, joint pain, anxiety, depression, stress, insomnia or sleep quality, digestion, blood pressure, circulation, hormonal function, fertility, pregnancy outcomes, immunity, inflammation, headaches, or any other health outcome.

Where a page on this site describes what a pose does, it describes physical mechanics only: which joints move and in which direction, which tissues lengthen, and what has to work to hold you in position. That is the limit of what we claim, and it is deliberate.

We also do not publish weight-loss content of any kind. There are no calorie figures on this site, no fat-burning or toning language, no body-transformation or before-and-after content, no commentary on body shape, and no diet, cleanse, fasting or detox material. Yoga is not presented here as a way of changing how a body looks.

Consult a professional

3. Consult a professional before you start

Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning any new physical practice. This is particularly important if any of the following applies to you:

  • You are pregnant, may be pregnant, or are in the postpartum period.
  • You have had surgery recently, or have any post-surgical restriction on movement.
  • You have a current or recent injury, including any injury to the spine, neck, shoulders, wrists, hips, knees or ankles.
  • You are managing a cardiovascular condition, high or low blood pressure, or a heart condition of any kind.
  • You have a respiratory condition, including asthma.
  • You have a neurological condition, a seizure disorder, a history of stroke, or a history of fainting or dizziness.
  • You have osteoporosis, osteopenia, a disc condition, or any diagnosed joint condition.
  • You have glaucoma, a retinal condition, or have had a recent eye procedure.
  • You have an inner ear condition or any condition affecting balance.
  • You take medication that affects blood pressure, balance, alertness or coordination.
  • You are recovering from illness, or have any condition that limits exertion.

If you are unsure whether something applies to you, that is itself a reason to ask. A short conversation with a professional who can examine you is worth more than any amount of reading.

4. Stop if you feel pain

Come out of any pose that hurts. Not gradually, not after the count finishes, not once the pose is over. Immediately.

Sensation, effort and pain are three different things, and telling them apart is most of the skill:

  • Sensation is broad, spread over the belly of a muscle, and eases as you keep breathing.
  • Effort is muscular work. A leg shakes. The breath wants to shorten. It is a signal to hold for less time.
  • Pain is sharp, hot, electrical or pinching, is often located in a joint rather than a muscle, and does not ease as you breathe. It is a stop signal, not a challenge to work through.

Other reasons to stop immediately: dizziness, lightheadedness, faintness, chest pain or tightness, shortness of breath, nausea, numbness or tingling in the hands or feet, a change in vision, or pressure in the head. Sit or lie down, breathe normally, and do not return to the practice that session.

If you experience chest pain, severe shortness of breath, sudden severe headache, weakness or numbness on one side, or any symptom you believe may be a medical emergency, stop and seek emergency medical care. In the United States, call 911.

Pain is a stop signal, not a challenge to work through.

Breathing practices

5. Breathing practices

The breathing patterns described on this site, including the breath-pacing bar that appears under the header on every page, are optional. Breath retention in particular is a real physiological event and is not appropriate for everyone.

Do not practice breath retention, and speak with a qualified healthcare professional before practicing any breathing technique, if you are pregnant, have a respiratory or cardiovascular condition, high or low blood pressure, a history of fainting, a history of panic, or a seizure disorder.

Never practice a breathing technique while driving, operating machinery, in or near water, or while standing on one leg. Stop immediately if you feel dizzy, lightheaded, short of breath, tingly in the hands or face, or anxious.

6. Work with a qualified teacher

Written instruction has real limits. A page cannot see whether your front knee is tracking inward, whether your shoulders have crept toward your ears, or whether the shape you have arrived at resembles the one described. A teacher in the room can.

We recommend working with a qualified teacher in person, particularly in your first months of practice and particularly before attempting anything labeled advanced on this site. The teacher directory is a starting point and includes a list of questions worth asking before you book. Note that Stillform does not vet, endorse or certify anyone listed there, and that verifying credentials is your responsibility.

7. Assumption of risk

Physical practice carries an inherent risk of injury. By using the instructions on this site you accept that risk and acknowledge that you practice at your own discretion and responsibility.

You are responsible for judging whether a given pose or sequence is appropriate for you on a given day, for practicing on a stable and adequate surface with enough clear space, for using props that are in good condition, and for stopping when something does not feel right.

To the fullest extent permitted by law, you release Stillform, its owners, contributors and contractors from liability for any injury, loss or damage arising from your use of this site. This section should be read together with sections 12 and 13 of the Terms of Use.

8. Children

This site is not directed to anyone under 13 and is written for adults. Nothing here should be used to instruct a child in a physical practice without the supervision of a qualified teacher and the involvement of a parent or guardian.

9. Accuracy and corrections

We work carefully and we still get things wrong sometimes. If something on this site is inaccurate, unclear, or unsafe as written, please tell us: hello@stillform.co or through the contact form. We reply within two business days, we fix errors, and we add a dated note to the page recording what changed. Corrections are not quietly edited in.
