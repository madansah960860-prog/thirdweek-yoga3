10 minutes / Beginner

Ten Minutes, First Thing

Ten poses, finished before the coffee is made. The spine moves in every direction it has, the front of the hips opens after a night flat on a bed, and weight goes back through the hands and feet. You need a wall, a folded blanket, and about the floor space of a bath towel.

Timed order

Pose-by-pose order

  1. Seated centering Sukhāsana Sit on the folded blanket so the hips end up higher than the knees. Hands wherever they are comfortable. Inhale four, exhale four, and let the minute be a minute.
    60 seconds
  2. Cat and Cow Mārjaryāsana and Bitilāsana On hands and knees. Inhale and let the belly descend as the chest opens. Exhale and round the spine from the tail up. One round per breath, which is about eleven rounds in the time given.
    90 seconds
  3. Child's Pose Bālāsana Knees as wide as the mat, hips travelling back toward the heels, forehead on the floor. If the forehead does not reach, stack a block under it rather than lifting the hips. Full guide.
    60 seconds
  4. Downward-Facing Dog Adho Mukha Śvānāsana Bend the knees more than feels necessary. A long spine with bent knees is the pose. Straight legs with a rounded back is not. Heels do not need to reach the floor, this morning or ever. Full guide.
    60 seconds
  5. Low Lunge, right foot forward Añjaneyāsana Put the blanket under the back knee before you step in, not after. Front foot flat, hips settling forward, chest staying up. Hands on blocks or resting on the front thigh.
    60 seconds
  6. Low Lunge, left foot forward Añjaneyāsana Same setup, second side. The two sides will not match. That is worth noticing and not worth correcting before breakfast.
    60 seconds
  7. Standing Forward Fold Uttānāsana Feet hip-width, knees soft enough that the fold happens at the hips. Let the head hang and the jaw go slack. Hands on blocks, on the shins, or holding opposite elbows.
    60 seconds
  8. Mountain Pose, arms overhead Tāḍāsana Roll up slowly, then stand still with the weight even across both feet. Reach up without letting the shoulders climb toward the ears or the lower ribs push forward.
    45 seconds
  9. Tree Pose, right foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana Stand beside the wall with two fingertips resting on it. The lifted foot goes to the ankle, the calf or the inner thigh, and never onto the side of the knee. Full guide.
    60 seconds
  10. Tree Pose, left foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana Second side, same two fingertips. When the timer runs out, put both feet down and stand there for a few breaths before you walk off and start the day.
    45 seconds

Every body

Beginner modification path

If getting onto the floor is not happening today, run this column instead. Same ten poses, same order, same hold times. It is a different route through the sequence, not a smaller one.

  • Seated centering: sit on a chair with both feet flat instead of on the floor.
  • Cat and Cow: do it seated on the chair, hands on the thighs, rounding and opening the spine from there.
  • Child's Pose: stay on the chair and fold forward over a cushion held on your lap, forehead resting on it.
  • Downward-Facing Dog: take the wall version. Hands flat on the wall at hip height, feet walked back until the torso is horizontal.
  • Low Lunge: stand facing a chair, put one foot up on the seat, step the other foot back, and keep both hands on the chair back.
  • Standing Forward Fold: sit on the chair and fold forward over the thighs with the hands sliding down the shins.
  • Mountain Pose: do it seated, reaching the arms up from the chair.
  • Tree Pose: take the seated version, crossing one ankle over the opposite thigh and letting that knee fall open to the side.

01 / Pacing

Three places it speeds up

Where people rush, and what it costs

A short practice tempts you to move at the speed of the clock instead of the speed of the breath. In this order it happens in the same three places nearly every time, and each one has a specific cost.

Cat and Cow

Ninety seconds at breath speed is about eleven rounds. Hurried, it becomes thirty small rocks of the pelvis and then a wait for the timer. The spine warms at the pace of the breath, not the pace of the arms, so speeding it up costs you most of what the pose was there to do. Let one inhale carry the whole opening and one exhale carry the whole rounding. If you run out of movement before you run out of breath, slow down further.

The two Low Lunges

The blanket goes under the back knee before you step in. Most people step in first, feel a bare kneecap on a hard floor, then spend fifteen of their sixty seconds shuffling a blanket into place with one hand. Fold it and set it down while you are still in Child's Pose. The lunge then gets its full minute rather than three quarters of one.

Tree Pose

This is the pose people quietly drop when they are running late, and it is the only one of the ten that asks you to stand on one leg. Balance is trained by standing on one leg badly, often, for years. Keep two fingertips on the wall and put the foot on the ankle if that is where it sits today. A wobbly minute against a wall counts. A skipped minute does not.

The last ten seconds

The sequence ends with a pose, and then it ends again when you stand still with both feet down. That second ending is where most people are already walking to the kitchen. Stay for three breaths. It is the difference between having practiced and having completed a list, and it is the part you will notice missing at eleven in the morning.

  • Cat and Cow Move at breath speed, not arm speed.
  • Low Lunge Blanket under the back knee before you step in.
  • Tree Pose Two fingertips on the wall beats leaving it out.

What keeps a morning practice alive is not willpower. It is the mat already unrolled and the sheet of paper already lying next to it.

02 / In practice

Field notes

Notes on practicing this one

Ten minutes is a real length, not a shortened one. It is long enough to move every joint this sequence sets out to move, and short enough that you will still do it on a Thursday when the morning has gone wrong. Four short practices in a week leave more behind than one long one on a Sunday.

Practice before you eat if the morning allows it. Forward folds and lunges sit better on an empty stomach. If breakfast has to come first, leave thirty to sixty minutes and keep it light.

You are stiffer now than you will be at six this evening. Spinal discs take on fluid overnight, so most people have less range first thing than at any other hour. Bend the knees in the folds. Do not measure this morning against last night, and let the opening two poses be genuinely gentle rather than gentle in name.

Print it and leave it on the floor. Use your browser's print command on this page. A sheet of paper at the edge of the mat removes the decision of what comes next, and it keeps a phone out of a ten-minute window that was never going to survive one.

When this stops feeling like an occasion, the forty-minute gentle full practice is the next thing to read. The desk reset fits into the middle of a working day and needs no mat at all.
