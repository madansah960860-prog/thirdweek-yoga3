Twenty Minutes to Wind Down

Ten holds of one to three minutes, every one of them propped, every one of them on the floor. Nothing asks for strength and nothing asks for balance. The exhale runs twice as long as the inhale from the first pose to the last, and the practice finishes with your legs resting up a wall.

Set up before you start

Lay every prop down within arm's reach of the mat before the first pose begins. When the holds run this long, standing up to go and find a block is the single most reliable way to end a practice early. You want the bolster, the block, the blanket, and a clear patch of wall about three feet wide with nothing leaning against it.

Timed order

Pose-by-pose order

  1. Supported Child's Pose Bālāsana Lay the bolster lengthwise between wide knees and rest the whole torso along it, head turned to one side. Turn the head the other way when the timer passes halfway, or the neck will remember this in the morning. Full guide.
    3 minutes
  2. Seated side bend, right Pārśva Sukhāsana Sit cross-legged on the folded blanket. Right forearm or hand down beside you, left arm reaching up and over the top. Keep both sitting bones on the blanket, which will limit how far you go and should.
    60 seconds
  3. Seated side bend, left Pārśva Sukhāsana Change which shin sits in front before you repeat it. Reach long rather than lean far. The side of the ribs is what you are after, not the floor.
    60 seconds
  4. Reclined figure four, right Supta Kapotāsana On your back, right ankle crossed over the left thigh. Hold behind the left thigh with both hands, or loop the strap around it so the arms are not working for two minutes. Head and shoulders stay on the floor.
    2 minutes
  5. Reclined figure four, left Supta Kapotāsana Second side. If the hip has a great deal to say about it, leave the bottom foot on the floor and simply let the top knee fall open. That is the same pose with less of it.
    2 minutes
  6. Supported Bridge Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana Knees bent, feet flat. Lift the hips, slide the block under the bony part of the pelvis at its lowest height, then release your whole weight onto it. If anything is still working to hold you up, the block is in the wrong place. Full guide.
    3 minutes
  7. Reclined twist, right Supta Matsyendrāsana Draw both knees in, then let them travel to the right onto the bolster, either under them or between them. If the left shoulder lifts away from the floor, add height under the knees until it settles back down.
    90 seconds
  8. Reclined twist, left Supta Matsyendrāsana Second side, same support. Turning the head away from the knees is optional and always has been. Leave it facing the ceiling if the neck would rather.
    90 seconds
  9. Legs up the wall Viparīta Karaṇī Sit sideways against the wall, then swing the legs up as you lie back. Hips a few inches out from the wall, folded blanket under the head, arms open. There is nothing to do here and that is the instruction.
    3 minutes
  10. Final rest Śavāsana Slide away from the wall and lie flat. Legs long, feet falling open, arms a little away from the sides with the palms up. Stop counting the breath and let it find its own length again.
    2 minutes

Every body

Beginner modification path

A long hold asks for comfort rather than capability. Every change below adds support, so that being still for three minutes is actually possible rather than merely instructed.

  • Supported Child's Pose: if kneeling is not available, lie on your back with your calves resting on a chair seat instead, knees bent at ninety degrees.
  • Seated side bend: sit on a chair rather than the floor, both feet flat, and make the same reach from there.
  • Reclined figure four: loop a strap around the bottom thigh so the arms are not holding for two minutes. Or leave the bottom foot on the floor entirely.
  • Supported Bridge: swap the block for a bolster or a stack of two folded blankets if the block feels hard under the pelvis. If lying on your back is not comfortable at all, give the time to two more minutes of the reclined twist.
  • Reclined twist: slide the bolster fully under both knees so the legs are carried the whole way across. Nothing should be held up by you.
  • Legs up the wall: if getting the legs onto the wall is awkward, rest the calves on a chair seat instead. It is a genuine substitute and not a smaller one.
  • Final rest: put a bolster or a rolled blanket under the knees. Most people are more comfortable that way, which makes it the standard setup rather than an accommodation.
Health and safety

This sequence is general movement education. It is not medical advice, it does not treat, prevent or manage any health condition, and it makes no claim about your sleep. Talk to a qualified healthcare professional before you start a new physical practice, and particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition. If you are pregnant, long stretches lying flat on the back are generally not advised, so take the resting poses propped on your left side instead. If something hurts, come out of it. 01 / Setup

Before the first hold

How to set the room up in ninety seconds

A propped practice is mostly logistics. Ninety seconds of arranging beforehand is what lets twenty minutes run without a single interruption, and every item below has ended somebody's practice by being in the wrong place.

Clear the wall first

You need about three feet of wall with nothing leaning on it and nothing hanging above it. Do this before you unroll anything, because it is the one step that involves moving furniture and the one you will not want to do seventeen minutes in, lying on your back, with a block under your pelvis.

Stack the props at the top of the mat

Bolster, block, blanket, strap if you use one, in the order you will reach for them. Every one of them should be gettable from lying down. The moment you have to stand up to fetch something, the practice restarts from cold and usually does not.

Put the room one layer warmer than feels right

Body temperature drops once you stop moving, and a person who is slightly cold cannot settle into a three-minute hold. Wear more than you think you need, or fold a second blanket at the end of the mat to pull over you for the last two poses.

Take the light down, take the phone out

A lamp rather than the overhead. The phone face down in another room, or on do not disturb if you are using the timer on this page. If you want the pacer, start it before you lie down, because reaching up to press a button at minute fourteen undoes the pose you were in.

  • Props Bolster, block, blanket, all within reach of lying down.
  • Wall Three clear feet, cleared before you unroll the mat.
  • Warmth One layer more than the room seems to need.

If a muscle is working to keep you in the shape, the shape is not finished. Put something underneath it.

The single rule that governs all ten holds
02 / In practice

Field notes

Notes on practicing this one

A long hold is a different skill, not a longer version of a short one. Three minutes in one shape is several times anything in the morning sequence, and it does not feel like more of the same. Somewhere around ninety seconds most people meet a strong wish to move. Notice it before you act on it. Then act on it anyway if the sensation is sharpening rather than settling, because that is the difference that matters.

Support is the point, not a concession. In an active practice a little muscular holding belongs in the pose. Here it is the thing you are trying to remove. Anything you can stay in for three minutes without effort is what this sequence is asking for, and props are how you get there.

The long exhale is the whole breath instruction. Inhale four, exhale eight, from the first pose to the last. If eight strains, use six and stop negotiating with it. The pacing bar under the header on this page is already set to the pattern. More on breath counting.

Twenty minutes is not a reward for a good day. The evenings this sequence was written for are the ones where it feels like too much trouble. Legs up the wall and a blanket is a complete practice on its own if that is all you have in you tonight.

If mornings are not available to you, this sequence and the eight-minute desk reset make a full week between them, with no early starts anywhere in it.
