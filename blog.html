Editorial standards

How these pieces get written

A pose page can tell you what to do with your back foot. It cannot tell you that week three goes flat, that your left hip will behave differently from your right, or that the block on the shelf is equipment rather than a concession. That is the work these articles do.

They are written by the same small group that writes the pose guides. Each one is dated on the page and carries a revision date when the text changes. Corrections get made in the open rather than quietly absorbed. If something here is wrong, write to us and it gets fixed, with a dated line on the page naming what changed.

Health and safety

Everything published here is general movement education, not medical advice. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition. Stop if you feel pain. Read the full disclaimer.

01 / Where the line sits

Two short lists

What we publish, and what we will not

Most writing in this category is built on a promise about your body. We would rather set out both lists here than let you work them out by reading.

  • Byline Stillform editorial, the same people who write every pose guide on this site.
  • Dates Each article shows the day it was published and the day it was last revised.
  • Corrections Fixed in place, with a dated note naming the change.
  • Payment An article paid for by a brand says so on the first line, above the headline, before you read a word of it.

What gets published

  • Accounts of what practising is actually like, measured in weeks rather than minutes
  • Arguments about method, marked as opinion where they are opinion
  • Plain explanations of why a cue exists and what it is doing
  • Structures for fitting practice into a week that is already full
  • Corrections to things we published and got wrong

What never does

  • Claims that yoga treats, cures, prevents or manages any condition
  • Weight loss framing, calorie figures and before-and-after photographs
  • Commentary on what a body should look like or be able to reach
  • Diet, cleanse, fasting and detox material of any kind
  • Paid placement that is not labelled as paid placement

An article that promises what your body will look like in thirty days is selling something. We would rather tell you what week three feels like.
