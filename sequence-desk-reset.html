Eight Minutes at the Desk

Twelve movements from a chair and from standing beside it, in the clothes you already have on. Nothing touches the floor and nothing needs a mat. This is the practice for the part of a working day when you have eight minutes and no private room to spend them in.

Before you start

Find a chair that will not roll or spin. If yours has castors, back it against a wall, lock them, or borrow a chair from the meeting room. Two of the twelve movements shift your weight forward over your feet, and a seat that slides away underneath you is the one genuine hazard in an otherwise very ordinary eight minutes.

Sequence timer

Each hold counts down and the next movement starts on its own. Tap any row below to send the timer there. Nothing here should reach the point of strain, so if one does, leave it and let the timer carry on without you.

Timed order

Pose-by-pose order

  1. Seated tall breathing Move forward on the seat until your back is clear of the chair back. Both feet flat, knees stacked over the ankles. Inhale four counts, exhale six. Nothing else happens yet.
    45 seconds
  2. Neck release, right side Let your right ear travel toward your right shoulder and let the left arm hang heavy off the side of the chair. The weight of the arm is the whole stretch. No hand on the head.
    35 seconds
  3. Neck release, left side Second side. Keep both sitting bones evenly weighted on the seat, or the whole torso leans and the neck gets nothing.
    35 seconds
  4. Shoulder rolls Slow circles, as large as the shoulder will make them. Roughly five going backward, then five going forward. Keep the breath even rather than holding it while you concentrate.
    30 seconds
  5. Seated Cat and Cow Mārjaryāsana and Bitilāsana Hands on your knees, elbows soft. Inhale and open the chest as the belly descends. Exhale and round the spine, letting the head drop last.
    45 seconds
  6. Seated twist, right Ardha Matsyendrāsana Both feet stay flat and both sitting bones stay down. Turn from the base of the spine upward, so the lower back goes first and the head goes last. The hands rest where they land. They are not a lever.
    40 seconds
  7. Seated twist, left Ardha Matsyendrāsana Second side, same restraint with the hands. If the knees have drifted apart while you were turning, square them up before you start.
    40 seconds
  8. Seated figure four, right Supta Kapotāsana, seated Cross the right ankle over the left thigh and let that knee fall open. Sit tall first. Then hinge forward from the hips, stopping at the first clear sensation rather than the last one.
    45 seconds
  9. Seated figure four, left Supta Kapotāsana, seated Second side. Keep the spine long as you hinge, so the movement stays in the hip. If the ankle will not cross the thigh, rest it on the shin and stay upright.
    45 seconds
  10. Chair-supported forward fold Uttānāsana, seated Take the feet wide and fold forward between the knees with the arms and head loose. Come back up slowly, stacking one vertebra at a time, and expect it to take longer than going down did.
    45 seconds
  11. Standing chest opener at the desk Ardha Adho Mukha Śvānāsana Stand up, put both hands flat on the edge of the desk and walk your feet back until the torso is horizontal and the arms are straight. Let the chest descend between the arms. Push the desk away rather than hanging off it.
    45 seconds
  12. Seated closing breath Sit back down, feet flat, hands anywhere. Inhale four counts, exhale six, about four times through. Then go back to what you were doing without ceremony.
    30 seconds

Every body

Beginner modification path

The sequence is already built around a chair, so nothing here changes position. Every change below reduces range, and none of them shortens the practice.

  • Neck releases: reduce the tilt by half. Neck range varies enormously between people and there is no angle you are aiming for.
  • Seated Cat and Cow: keep the movement small and let most of it happen in the upper back if the lower back is sensitive today.
  • Seated twist: keep both hands on your own thighs rather than reaching for the chair. The turn should come from turning, not from pulling on furniture.
  • Seated figure four: if the ankle will not cross the opposite thigh, rest it on the shin, or simply let one knee fall open with the foot still on the floor. Skip the forward hinge altogether if the hip is sensitive.
  • Chair forward fold: fold only to horizontal, forearms resting on your thighs. Come back up on an inhale.
  • Desk chest opener: stand closer to the desk so the torso sits at forty-five degrees rather than horizontal. Leave it out entirely if you have shoulder pain.
Health and safety

This sequence is general movement education. It is not medical advice, and it does not treat, prevent or manage any health condition, including anything you may have read about sitting. Talk to a qualified healthcare professional before you start a new physical practice, and particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition. 01 / Short version

When the meeting runs over

What to do if you only have half the time

Four minutes is a real option, and it is a much better option than deciding there is no point. Do not speed the movements up to fit twelve into four. Keep six of them at their stated holds and leave the other six out.

  • Keep Seated tall breathing, seated Cat and Cow, both seated twists, the standing desk opener, the closing breath.
  • Drop The neck releases, the shoulder rolls, both figure fours, the chair forward fold.
  • Never split A pose done on the right is done on the left. Drop a pair, never one side of one.

That keeps the two things the short version exists for: the spine moving in more than one plane, and the front of the chest opening after an hour of reaching forward for a keyboard. It also keeps the opening and closing breath, which are what make the four minutes feel like a practice rather than an interruption.

The cost is the hips. Both figure fours go, and they are the only movements in the twelve that address the outside of the hip. If that is the part of you that is loud by mid-afternoon, swap them in for the two twists instead. Same count, same symmetry, ten seconds longer.

There is a shorter version still, under three minutes, and it is short enough to say in one line: the opening breath, seated Cat and Cow, the standing desk opener, the closing breath.

Nothing in this sequence asks you to explain yourself to the person at the next desk.

The design constraint that shaped every movement in it
02 / In practice

Field notes

Notes on practicing this one

It is built to look like nothing. No floor, no change of clothes, no moment where a colleague has to decide whether to say something. The two standing movements read as stretching at a desk because that is exactly what they are. In an open office that constraint mattered more than any other while this was being written.

Twice a day beats once a week. Eight minutes at eleven and eight more at three is a different proposition from forty minutes on a Saturday, and it survives a calendar far better. It also breaks up a long sitting day twice instead of once.

Set the chair up before the timer starts. Feet flat, knees roughly level with the hips, back clear of the chair back. If your feet do not reach the floor, put a box or a stack of books under them. If the seat sits too low, fold a jacket and sit on it. Four seconds of setup changes every movement that follows.

Shoes are fine. Bare feet are slightly better. Everything here works in shoes. If you can slip them off for the standing desk opener the feet read the floor better, but nothing in the twelve depends on it.

If the middle of the day is the only window you can reliably protect, this sequence and the evening wind-down make a complete week between them, with no early mornings anywhere in it.
